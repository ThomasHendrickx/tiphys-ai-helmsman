## 2. The decision the plan left open: `head` is REQUIRED

Stated first, with its cost, because the dispatch asked for the decision and
not for a survey.

**REQUIRED, `type: string`, `pattern: ^[0-9a-f]{40}$`.** The edit is at
schemas/verdict.schema.json:12 (the `required` list) and
schemas/verdict.schema.json:33 (the property).

The argument is one sentence and the rest is consequence: **an OPTIONAL head
leaves the old convention in force for every document that omits it, and every
document omits it.** Before this phase the only thing tying a pair of reviews to
one commit was the DIRECTORY the operator pointed the gate at, a reading
declared in delivery/work-history/m3-p9.md:1 and enforced by nothing. If `head`
is optional, the check must keep a fallback for head-less verdicts, that
fallback is the directory, and the directory is exactly what
delivery/decisions/DR-0012-delegated-merge-authority.md:22 was being satisfied
by assertion rather than by check. Optional would ship the field and keep the
hazard. It would also redden criterion 2, which asks for exit 1 naming `head`.

**Full length rather than abbreviated, for a reason that is about the check and
not about tidiness.** A short sha and a full sha are two spellings of ONE fact,
and nothing here can reconcile them. The check's whole input is an instance and
a context DIRECTORY, src/checks.ts:92, so resolving a prefix against a commit
graph is not something it has the arguments to attempt. Two spellings would
therefore form two groups of one, and a group of one is refused rather than
compared, at src/checks.ts:3699 and src/checks.ts:3916; the captured
criterion-3 run in section 5 shows both refusals firing on one directory.

So the abbreviation is refused at the document boundary
(schemas/verdict.schema.json:33) and refused again inside the check
(src/checks.ts:3295), and the two layers agree rather than one relying on the
other. Case is refused by the pattern and ALSO folded by `establishField`, so an
upper-case sha is one key with its lower-case spelling rather than a second
group; both directions are witnessed, the refusal at test/verdict-head.test.ts:642
and the folding at test/verdict-head.test.ts:666.

**What required cost, exactly.** Five files migrated and two left alone:

## 3. What changed, and the mechanism behind each change

### 3a. The schema: `head`, and the escalation widening

The escalation `contains` enum went from `["high", "critical"]` to
`["medium", "high", "critical"]` (schemas/verdict.schema.json:101). The
mechanism, stated as a mechanism rather than as the finding:
**a predicate written in two places drifted, and the narrower copy is the one
that decides.** delivery/decisions/DR-0012-delegated-merge-authority.md:23 bars
an unresolved finding at high or MEDIUM; the schema stopped at `high`. So a
verdict reading APPROVE beside a finding the review itself ranked medium
validated at exit 0, and the merge precondition the schema exists to carry was
one severity wider than the schema.

`low` is deliberately still mergeable, which is what that same line of DR-0012
says, and a test asserts the low arm as a control so a widening that overshot
would redden.

The two places are now held equal by a test rather than by care:
test/verdict-head.test.ts:513 compares the schema's enum against
src/checks.ts:3775 (`BLOCKING_SEVERITIES`) as sets. That is the hazard row "the
medium widening in the schema only" and its mirror, in one assertion.

### 3b. The check: the join key is `(phase, head)`

`dual-review-decorrelation` grouped by `phase` alone. It now groups by
`(phase, head)` through src/checks.ts:3323.

**A sibling that cannot be keyed is a VIOLATION, not a skip**, and that is the
part worth reading. A `filter` would have dropped a same-phase verdict whose
head is unreadable, which SHRINKS the group; a shrinking group is the fail-open
shape this file has already been bitten by twice, at `loadCommittedVerdicts`
and at the `phase` canonicalisation, both recorded in its own comments. With
three verdicts, two of them sharing a model family, giving the third an
unreadable head would otherwise leave a compared pair of two and a green run.
So `headGroupFor` returns the members AND the refusals, and the caller seeds its
violation list with the refusals. The refusal is captured in section 5 as
member two of the criterion-3 class, where a third verdict carrying an empty
head reddens a directory whose other two verdicts are a good pair.

### 13.6 The fix

One reader, exported, used by both sites.

`readVerdictKind` in `src/checks.ts` returns THREE outcomes where the two sites
each had a boolean: `verdict` (a member, carrying the record), `other` (the
document answered, and the answer is not verdict), and `unreadable` (the `kind`

of what was found). `loadCommittedVerdicts` pushes `unreadable` onto the
existing `unexaminable` array, which both merge predicates already seed their
violations with, so no new plumbing is added between the loader and the
verdicts. `committedVerdictPaths` in `scripts/check-dual-review.mjs` drops its
own selection rule and calls the same function, which closes the divergence in
section 13.4's last two rows at the same time.

The exported reader is the mechanism-level half. The alternative was to add the
same three-outcome test at both sites, which is two readers of one fact again,
and the file itself records at `establishDelegatedRegime` that this was already
done once in this phase for exactly that reason.
